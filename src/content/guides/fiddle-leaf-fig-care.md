# Fiddle Leaf Fig Care: Healthy Leaves That Stay On

Fiddle leaf figs do best in bright, indirect light or 2 to 6 hours of direct sun, kept out of hot afternoon sun [1]. Water only when the top 5 cm (2 in) of soil is dry, and keep it at 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F) [2].

The fiddle leaf fig (*Ficus lyrata*) is a tropical tree in the fig family, Moraceae [1]. Its species name, *lyrata*, refers to its lyre-shaped leaves, which can reach 46 cm (18 in) long [1]. It grows wild in the lowland rainforests of tropical West and Central Africa, reaching 18 to 30 m (60 to 100 ft) [1][2]. Indoors it usually stays 60 cm to 3 m (2 to 10 ft) tall [1].

## Light

The New York Botanical Garden (NYBG) recommends strong light without direct rays on the leaves [2]. Use your brightest room, set back from the window or behind a sheer curtain [2]. NC State Extension also lists partial shade, meaning 2 to 6 hours of direct sun, with protection from afternoon sun [1]. Too much direct sun can scorch the leaves [1].

Keep the leaves dust-free, because dust blocks light [2]. Turn the pot a quarter turn each time you water, so all sides get equal light [2].

## Watering

Skip the calendar: water when the top 5 cm (2 in) of soil feels dry [2]. Water until it drains from the hole, then empty the saucer after about 15 minutes [2]. From October through February, lower light can slow water uptake, so you'll water less often [2]. Fiddle leaf figs are sensitive to overwatering, and roots rot easily in soil that stays wet [1][2].

## Temperature and Humidity

Keep it at a steady 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F), or about 18°C (65°F) in winter, changed gradually [2]. NC State says it performs best above 13°C (55°F) [1]. The plant reacts to temperature swings by dropping leaves, so keep it away from heating and cooling vents, open windows and doors [2].

Aim for humidity above 40%, which NYBG says prevents brown leaf tips [2]. Many homes drop to 10 to 20% humidity in winter, and Iowa State Extension calls humidifiers an excellent way to raise it [3]. Misting doesn't work: humidity rises for only a few minutes [3].

## Soil, Pots and Repotting

Use a soil-based potting mix with some added perlite, in a pot with a drainage hole [2][4]. In a decorative pot without a hole, use a plastic liner pot and empty any water that collects [2]. Skip the gravel layer. Illinois Extension calls it a myth, because water gathers in the soil just above the gravel [4].

Fiddle leaf figs like to be slightly root-bound and can stay in one pot for several years [2]. When roots show at the surface or through the hole, move up one pot size in spring [2].

## Feeding

Feed only in spring and summer, with a balanced organic fertilizer at half strength every other week [2]. Over-feeding can itself cause leaf drop [2].

## Leaf Drop and Brown Spots

Lower leaves that slowly yellow and fall are simply aging [2]. Sudden leaf drop is a stress signal [2]. Common triggers include the trip home from the nursery and drafts from heaters, air conditioners, doors or windows [2]. So can watering too much, too little or irregularly [1][2]. After a move, keep conditions steady and make other changes gradually [2].

Brown spots look different depending on the cause [2]:

- **Crisp, dry brown spots:** underwatering or irregular watering [2].
- **Soft, light-colored spots around the veins that turn brown:** too much water or poor drainage [2]. Check the roots for rot.
- **Tiny red or brown spots on new leaves:** soil staying too moist [2]. NYBG says these are ruptured cells and harmless if you correct watering quickly [2].

This last pattern fits edema, a disorder that occurs when roots take up more water than the leaves lose [5]. University of Maryland Extension describes edema as crusty, raised bumps on the undersides of leaves that may turn brown [5]. It advises letting the mix dry between waterings, especially when the mix is cool and the air warm [5].

Fungal and bacterial leaf spots also occur, but they often follow some other stress [2].

## Common Problems

- **Lower leaves yellow and drop one at a time:** normal aging [2]. If many turn yellow at once, check watering and light. See [yellow leaves](/guides/yellow-leaves).
- **Brown, dry leaf edges:** air too dry [2]. Raise humidity above 40% with a humidifier. See [brown leaf tips](/guides/brown-leaf-tips).
- **Soft spots and soil that stays wet:** overwatering or poor drainage [2]. Let the top 5 cm (2 in) dry and check for [root rot](/guides/root-rot).
- **Burned brown patches:** too much direct sun [1][2]. Move the plant back from the glass or add a sheer curtain.
- **Long, bare branches, or brown spots on shaded lower leaves:** too little light [2]. Move it somewhere brighter and prune leggy branches in spring.
- **Brown spots near a vent:** temperature swings from heating or cooling [1]. Move it away from the vent.
- **Raised white dots that don't wipe off:** lithocysts, normal leaf cells that hold mineral deposits [2]. No fix needed.
- **Insects on stems or leaves:** scale, aphids, mealybugs, thrips or spider mites [1]. Dusting leaves reduces mites [2]. For heavy infestations, ask a local nursery or plant clinic.

## Pruning and Propagation

In spring, cut leggy branches back to a node with a bud facing where you want growth [2]. A new branch usually forms just below the cut [2]. Leave at least two-thirds of the leaves to fuel recovery [2]. Wear gloves, because the milky sap can irritate skin [1]. NC State recommends stem cuttings for propagation [1].

## Common Questions

### Is a Fiddle Leaf Fig Toxic to Cats?

The ASPCA doesn't list *Ficus lyrata* by name. It lists the related *Ficus benjamina*, under "Fig," as toxic to cats, dogs and horses, causing digestive and skin irritation [6]. NC State lists fiddle leaf fig itself as a problem for cats and dogs [1]. If a pet chews it, call your vet or, in the US, the ASPCA Poison Control line at (888) 426-4435 [6].

### Should I Rotate My Fiddle Leaf Fig?

Yes. Give it a quarter turn each time you water, as NYBG suggests, so all sides get equal light and the shape stays balanced [2].

### How Often Should I Water a Fiddle Leaf Fig?

There's no fixed schedule. Water when the top 5 cm (2 in) of soil feels dry, and expect longer gaps from October through February [2].

### Why Is My Fiddle Leaf Fig Dropping Leaves?

Sudden leaf drop signals stress, often from the trip home from the nursery, drafts, temperature swings, or too much or too little water [1][2]. Keep it in one bright, steady spot and check the soil before watering.
