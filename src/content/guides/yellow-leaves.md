# Yellow Leaves on Houseplants: Causes and Fixes

The most common cause of yellow leaves on houseplants is overwatering, which reduces the oxygen available to the roots [1]. Check the mix first: if it feels wet 5 cm (2 in) down, hold off on water and make sure the pot drains [1, 4].

## Quick Diagnosis

Note which leaves yellow first, whether whole leaves or just the areas between veins turn yellow, and whether the plant wilts.

| What you see | Likely cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lower leaves yellow; mix stays wet | Overwatering or poor drainage | Let the top 5 cm (2 in) dry; empty the saucer |
| Wilting in wet mix; brown, soft roots | Root rot | Trim rotted roots and repot, or discard if most are dead |
| Wilting, gray-green leaves; dry mix shrinking from the pot | Underwatering | Water until it drains; check more often |
| Yellowing and leaf drop in a dim spot; stretched stems | Too little light | Move to a brighter spot, avoiding sudden direct sun |
| An occasional oldest leaf yellows; new growth is healthy | Natural aging | Nothing to fix |
| Older leaves turn pale yellow first; slow growth | Nitrogen shortage | Feed with a general-purpose liquid fertilizer |
| Yellow between veins on the newest leaves (iron) or older leaves (magnesium) | Iron or magnesium shortage | Fix watering first; then add iron or magnesium |
| Yellowing or leaf drop after a cold draft, a move or repotting | Chilling or shock | Keep above 10 °C (50 °F); allow about three weeks |
| Pale speckles, fine webbing, mites under the leaves | Spider mites | Isolate; rinse the leaves; use insecticidal soap if needed |

## Overwatering, Poor Drainage and Root Rot

**How to tell:** Older leaves yellow and may drop while the mix stays wet [3, 4]. Algae on the surface and a soft stem base also point to excess water [2, 3]. Wilting in wet mix suggests root rot, so check the roots [2]. Healthy roots are white, at least under their outer layer [2]. Rotted roots are brown to black and may be soft [1, 2].

**What to do:** Wait until the top 5 cm (2 in) is dry, then water until it drains and empty the saucer [4]. Use pots with drainage holes [6]. Old mix compacts and drains poorly, so repot if it stays soggy [2, 4]. Skip a gravel layer at the bottom: it doesn't improve drainage but raises the waterlogged zone closer to the roots [7].

If only a few roots have rotted, cut them out and repot in sterile mix [1]. If most roots are dark, soft and dead, discard the plant [1]. Our [root rot](/guides/root-rot) guide has more.

## Underwatering

**How to tell:** A dry plant wilts and turns gray-green [4]. If it stays dry too long, lower leaves yellow and die, and leaves may drop [1]. The pot feels light, and the mix may pull away from the pot's edge [3, 4].

**What to do:** Water thoroughly until it drains from the bottom, then check the mix more often [4]. If a plant needs water very often, or your finger can't get 5 cm (2 in) into the mix, it may be pot-bound [4, 6]. Move it to a pot about one-third wider and deeper [6].

## Too Little Light

**How to tell:** Too little light can yellow older or newer leaves and make them drop [2, 3]. New stems often stretch, with long gaps between leaves [2].

**What to do:** Move the plant somewhere brighter, such as nearer a window. Avoid a sudden move into hot, direct sun, which can sunburn leaves [2].

## Natural Aging of Older Leaves

**How to tell:** Old leaves can yellow from natural aging [3]. That's the likely reason when only the occasional oldest leaf yellows and the rest of the plant looks healthy.

**What to do:** Nothing needs fixing. Just clear fallen leaves from the pot [2].

## Nutrient Deficiencies

**How to tell:** Note where the yellowing starts [3, 5].

- **Nitrogen:** Older leaves turn pale yellow first, then the yellowing spreads to younger ones [5]. Pot-bound plants are especially prone [1].
- **Magnesium:** Older leaves yellow between the veins, sometimes with reddish-brown tints, then may brown and drop early [3, 5].
- **Iron:** The youngest leaves yellow between the veins [3, 5]. Alkaline conditions make iron harder for roots to take up [5].

Deficiencies are hard to tell apart, and drought, waterlogging and low light can mimic them [5]. Soggy mix also hinders uptake [5]. Rule out watering and light problems before you feed.

**What to do:** Nutrients in fresh potting mix last only about 6 to 8 weeks, so feed regularly with a general-purpose liquid fertilizer [5]. For iron, use a liquid feed with sequestered (chelated) iron [5]. For magnesium, the RHS suggests an Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate) leaf spray and warns that high-potassium feeds can cause the shortage [5].

Too much fertilizer can also yellow young leaves and cause wilting [3]. White crusty deposits on the mix or pot rim mean salts are building up [1]. Flush the pot with clear water every four to six months [4]. Salts can also cause [brown leaf tips](/guides/brown-leaf-tips) [2].

## Cold Drafts and Sudden Temperature Changes

**How to tell:** Exposure to low temperatures can yellow leaves and make them drop [1, 2]. Chilling below 10 °C (50 °F) can cause leaf spots, and cold damage can blacken leaves [2, 3]. Sudden temperature changes can also trigger leaf drop [2].

**What to do:** Move the plant away from cold windows, outside doors and drafts [2]. Keep it above 10 °C (50 °F) [2].

## Shock After Moving or Repotting

**How to tell:** Leaves yellow or drop soon after a move or repotting. Sudden changes in light, temperature or humidity can cause leaf drop [2], and some yellowing after repotting is normal [6].

**What to do:** Keep care steady. Leaf drop after a big change in surroundings should stop within about three weeks [1]. After repotting, give the plant indirect light for a few days and don't fertilize for at least two weeks [6].

## Spider Mites and Other Pests

**How to tell:** Spider mites leave tiny pale speckles, called stippling, and leaves later yellow, bronze and dry out [2]. Fine webbing may appear near growing tips [2]. The mites are about 0.5 mm (0.02 in) long and live mostly under the leaves, so use a 10x hand lens [2]. Dry, dusty indoor conditions favor them [2]. Whiteflies, mealybugs, scale and thrips can also yellow leaves [2].

**What to do:** Isolate the plant [2]. Wash the leaves often with water, including the undersides, and reduce dust [2]. If mites persist, use an insecticidal soap or horticultural oil labeled for indoor use on that plant [2]. Mites grow from egg to adult in about two weeks at 24 °C (75 °F), so keep checking [2]. Misting won't fix dry air: it raises humidity for only about 20 minutes. A humidifier works better [7].

## How to Prevent Yellow Leaves

- Water when the top 5 cm (2 in) is dry, not on a schedule; succulents and cacti need far less [4].
- Use pots with drainage holes, empty saucers and skip the gravel layer [4, 6, 7].
- Feed regularly, following the label, and flush the mix with clear water every four to six months [4, 5].
- Choose plants that suit your light and temperature, and keep them out of drafts [2].
- Check new plants for pests weekly for several weeks, then monthly [2].

## When to Get Help

Ask for help if yellowing keeps spreading after you fix watering, light and temperature, or if you can't identify a pest [2]. Soft, mushy stems with a bad smell can mean a bacterial disease, which is hard to control [2]. Take photos or a sample to a local nursery, plant clinic or, in the US, a county Cooperative Extension office [2]. Sometimes starting over with a new plant is the better choice [1, 2].

## Common Questions

### How Do I Tell Overwatering From Underwatering?

Feel the mix 5 cm (2 in) down and lift the pot [4]. Wet mix, a heavy pot or surface algae point to overwatering [3, 4]. Dry mix pulling from the pot's edge, a light pot and gray-green wilting point to underwatering [3, 4]. Both can make leaves yellow and drop [1].

### Why Are the Lower Leaves Turning Yellow?

Causes include overwatering, root rot, a pot-bound plant, nitrogen or magnesium shortage and natural aging [3]. If only an occasional old leaf yellows and new growth looks healthy, it's probably aging. If several yellow at once, check drainage and roots first [1].

### Why Are New Leaves Turning Yellow?

Yellow new leaves can point to low light, iron shortage or too much fertilizer [3]. Overwatering and a pH imbalance in the mix are other causes [2].

### What Do Yellow Leaves With Green Veins Mean?

Yellow between green veins is a typical sign of a nutrient shortage [5]. On the newest leaves it suggests iron, and on older leaves magnesium [3, 5]. Fix watering first, because waterlogged mix can cause similar signs [5].
