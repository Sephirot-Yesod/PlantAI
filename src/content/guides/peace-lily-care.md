# Peace Lily Care: Fewer Droops, Healthier Leaves and More Flowers

Water a peace lily when the top 2.5 cm (1 in) of potting mix feels dry, and check it every day or two [4]. Give it bright, indirect light but never direct sun, at 20 to 29°C (68 to 85°F) [1, 3].

Despite its name, the peace lily is not a true lily but a member of the arum family (Araceae), like anthuriums [3]. Its white "flower" is a spathe, a leaf-like hood around a spike of tiny true flowers [1, 2]. Wild species grow on humid tropical forest floors in Central and South America and Southeast Asia [1, 2]. Many houseplants are hybrid cultivars [3].

## Light

Peace lilies tolerate low light and even heavy shade, but grow best in bright, indirect light [1, 3]. Bright, indirect light casts a clear shadow, but no sun rays reach the leaves [4]. Direct sun scorches the foliage [3]. UF/IFAS recommends 1,500 to 2,500 foot-candles (about 16,000 to 27,000 lux) for greenhouse growing; more bleaches the leaves [2]. Light fades fast away from a window, and dim spots mean slow growth and few flowers [3, 4].

## Watering

Keep the mix moist but not soggy, letting it dry slightly between waterings [3]. Check it rather than watering on a fixed day, since needs change with light, warmth and season [4]. When it feels dry about 2.5 cm (1 in) down, water until it drains, and empty the saucer after 30 to 45 minutes [4]. In rooms above 27°C (80°F), keep the mix moist [2].

A droop in dry mix is a clear thirst signal. Even a full collapse, which growers call a "crash," reverses within hours of a thorough watering [2]. Don't wait for the droop, though: Missouri Botanical Garden advises against letting the plant wilt [1]. A severe wilt can leave yellow or dead patches, and frequent drooping leads to brown tips and edges [2].

Drinkable tap water suits most houseplants, though heavily softened water can build up salts [4]. Missouri Botanical Garden suggests room-temperature water left to stand so chlorine can evaporate [1].

## Temperature and Humidity

Peace lilies prefer 20 to 29°C (68 to 85°F) by day, with nights about 6°C (10°F) cooler [1, 3]. Keep them above 16°C (60°F) to avoid cold damage, and away from heating and air-conditioning vents [3].

Most tropical houseplants do best at 40 to 50 percent humidity, but homes can drop to 10 percent in winter [4]. Dry air can brown leaf tips and edges [4]. Misting raises humidity for only a few minutes and can spread disease [4]. A humidifier is very effective, and grouping plants or a pebble tray also helps; keep the pot above the tray's water [4].

## Soil, Pots and Repotting

Almost any well-drained potting mix works [3]. Use a pot with a drainage hole. A gravel layer does not replace the hole, and it creates a wetter zone that promotes root rot [4].

Peace lilies like to be slightly pot-bound [1, 3]. Repot when roots grow out of the drainage holes or circle the surface, ideally in late winter or early spring [3, 4]. Choose a pot only 2.5 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) larger, since an oversized pot stays too wet [3].

## Feeding

Peace lilies need very little fertilizer [1, 3]. In spring and summer, feed every 6 to 8 weeks with a balanced liquid fertilizer (such as 20-20-20) at quarter strength [3]. Feed little or none in fall and winter, or for 2 to 4 weeks after repotting [3, 4]. Overfeeding burns leaf tips and roots; if it happens, flush the mix with plenty of water [1, 2, 3].

## Getting It to Flower

Most cultivars bloom only between January and August [2]. Clemson says lack of flowering usually means a plant under a year old or too little light [3]. UF/IFAS adds that plants grown for 9 to 15 months usually bloom from February to April [2].

Growers widely spray gibberellic acid, a plant growth regulator, to bring plants into bloom year-round [2]. So a plant bought in bloom may not flower again until it matures and its season comes. Treated plants generally have smaller flowers, and growth regulators may not be legal everywhere [2].

If a mature plant won't bloom, move it closer to a window, out of direct sun [3, 4]. Each bloom lasts about a month indoors; trim spent ones promptly [2].

## Common Problems

- **Droops, mix dry:** thirst. Water until it drains, and it should revive within hours [2].
- **Droops, mix wet:** waterlogged roots, which may be sparse with black tips [2]. Water less often and use a mix with more air space [2]. See [root rot](/guides/root-rot).
- **Droops on hot, bright afternoons, mix moist:** normal above 27 to 29°C (80 to 85°F), though frequent drooping browns the tips [2].
- **Brown tips or edges:** overfeeding, too much drying, dry air, cold or strong light [2, 3, 4]. Flush the mix, feed less and water before it wilts. See [brown leaf tips](/guides/brown-leaf-tips).
- **Black patches spreading in from leaf tips:** chilling injury from drafts or long spells below 7°C (45°F) [2]. Move it somewhere warmer.
- **Pale, bleached or curled leaves, few flowers:** too much light or heat [2]. Move it out of direct sun and below 32°C (90°F) [2].
- **Yellow leaves:** too much drying, a severe wilt or cold winter mix [2, 3]. Yellow between green veins can mean magnesium deficiency [3]. See [yellow leaves](/guides/yellow-leaves).
- **Insects:** fungus gnats, mealybugs, spider mites or scale [3]. Wipe leaves with a damp cloth, and ask a local nursery or plant clinic about heavy infestations [2, 3].

## Dividing and Propagation

Divide a crowded peace lily when you repot [2, 4]. Gently loosen the roots and remove dead ones [3, 4]. Separate side shoots, each with its own roots, and pot them in fresh mix [2]. Water well, then wait 2 to 4 weeks before feeding [4].

## Common Questions

### Is a Peace Lily Toxic to Cats?

Yes, but usually mildly: the ASPCA says chewing releases insoluble calcium oxalate crystals that can cause mouth pain, drooling, vomiting or diarrhea [6]. Far more dangerous are true lilies (Lilium) and daylilies (Hemerocallis): even small amounts can cause severe kidney injury in cats [6]. With those, treatment delayed past 18 hours is often fatal; call your vet or, in the US, ASPCA Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 [6].

### Why Is My Peace Lily Drooping?

Usually it is thirsty, and a thorough watering revives it within hours; if the mix is wet, the roots may be waterlogged instead [2]. Avoid repeated wilting, which yellows or browns the leaf edges [2, 3].

### Can a Peace Lily Live in Low Light?

Yes, it tolerates low light and even heavy shade [1, 3]. It grows best in bright, indirect light, though, and too little light often stops flowering [3].

### Does a Peace Lily Clean the Air?

Not measurably in a home. NASA's 1989 study found plants removed chemicals in small sealed chambers, but homes constantly exchange air and gain new pollutants [5]. Iowa State says even one plant per square foot (about 11 per square meter) cannot clear these chemicals effectively [5].
